RSS vs Apple Podcasts Scraper

When a public RSS feed is enough, and when Apple Podcasts iTunes search, charts, and watchlists help.

RSS is enough for known shows

If you already have a feed URL, RSS is the direct source for that show’s published catalog:

  • Episode titles, descriptions, and publish dates
  • Audio enclosures
  • Publisher-declared metadata (title, author, artwork, categories)

Use RSS to watch cadence and new episodes on shows you already track. A feed does not search Apple’s directory, and it does not include chart rank.

Apple catalog data helps for search, charts, and watchlists

The Apple Podcasts Scraper reads public Apple/iTunes endpoints for discovery and catalog IDs. It is a source step, not an RSS archive replacement, and it is not affiliated with Apple.

  • Search — find shows by keyword with the official iTunes Search API. RSS has no catalog search.
  • Charts — snapshot official rankings by country (shows or episodes). Rank is not in a show’s feed.
  • Watchlists — refresh known shows by Apple collection ID and keep stable Apple IDs and storefront URLs.

Storefront matters: the same query can differ across us, gb, jp, and other markets.

Optional recent episodes are available when you need a short look at what a discovered show is publishing. For a full episode history of a known feed, read RSS directly.

When is a public RSS feed enough for a known show?

If you already have a feed URL, RSS is the direct source for that show’s published catalog: episode titles, descriptions, and publish dates; audio enclosures; and publisher-declared metadata (title, author, artwork, categories). Use RSS to watch cadence and new episodes on shows you already track.

Does a public RSS feed search Apple Podcasts or include chart rank?

No. A feed does not search Apple’s directory, and it does not include chart rank. RSS also does not return collection IDs for other shows. Keyword discovery, official rankings, and collection-ID refresh are Apple catalog jobs, not feed jobs.

When should I use Apple Podcasts Scraper instead of RSS?

Use Apple Podcasts Scraper when you need Apple catalog search, a chart snapshot, or a watchlist refresh of known collection IDs. It reads public Apple/iTunes endpoints for discovery and catalog IDs. It is a source step, not an RSS archive replacement, and it is not affiliated with Apple.

Does Apple Podcasts Scraper replace a show’s RSS archive?

No. Optional recent episodes are a short look at what a discovered show is publishing. For a full episode history of a known feed, read RSS directly. Published scraper inputs are searchTerm, chartRequests, and lookupIds — not a public RSS URL field.

How do I search Apple Podcasts when I only have a topic?

Use keyword search. RSS has no catalog search. Apple Podcasts Scraper sets searchTerm against the official iTunes Search API. Storefront matters: the same query can differ across us, gb, jp, and other markets.

How do watchlists differ from reading RSS?

A watchlist refresh re-resolves known shows by Apple collection ID through the official iTunes Lookup API and keeps stable Apple IDs and storefront URLs. RSS watches cadence on a feed you already have. Use lookupIds when you have collection IDs; use RSS when you have a feed URL and need that show’s published catalog.

When should I use RSS & Atom Feed Extractor instead?

When you already have publisher feed URLs and need those feeds parsed into item URL lists. RSS & Atom Feed Extractor is not Apple directory search and does not snapshot Apple chart rank. Use Apple Podcasts Scraper for searchTerm, chartRequests, or lookupIds.

Can I pass a feed URL into Apple Podcasts Scraper?

No. Published inputs are searchTerm, chartRequests, and lookupIds. RSS is used internally when includeEpisodes is true. If you already have a public feed URL and need that show’s published catalog, read the feed directly or use RSS & Atom Feed Extractor.

How is Apple Podcasts Scraper priced on this split?

Billing is pay-per-event. The live Store card is from $10.00 / 1,000 results. Published events are Actor Start at $0.005 and result at $0.01 per default-dataset item ($10.00 per 1,000). Public RSS you fetch yourself is not this Actor’s priced event.
