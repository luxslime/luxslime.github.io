CLP label element checker

Direct answer. Paste the text of a CLP label or a product page, for example Danger. H225 Highly flammable liquid and vapour. GHS02. Acme GmbH, Hauptstraße 1, 10115 Berlin, +49 30 123456. 500 ml. The page shows which label elements of Article 17(1) of Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008 your text seems to mention and which it does not, each as a question to check. It does not classify the product or say whether the label complies. It runs in your browser and sends nothing anywhere.

This is a reading aid, not legal advice and not what any app checks. To create hazard profiles with GHS pictograms and statements, show them on product pages and review missing hazard fields before publishing, try CLPLens: CLP Hazard Display (Shopify App Store).

Where the questions come from

The ten rows follow Article 17(1)(a) to (h) of Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008 (CLP): supplier name, address and telephone number; nominal quantity for packages made available to the general public; product identifiers (Article 18); and, where applicable, hazard pictograms (Article 19, Annex V codes GHS01 to GHS09), signal word (Article 20), hazard statements (Article 21), precautionary statements (Article 22) and supplemental information (Article 25). The extra prompts come from Article 17(2) on languages, Article 20(3) on “Danger” and “Warning”, Article 26(1) on pictogram precedence, Article 29(1) on small packs and Article 48(2) on advertising. Article 48a (distance sales offers) was added by Regulation (EU) 2024/2865 and, under Regulation (EU) 2025/2439, applies from 1 January 2028.

The page looks for words, codes and patterns in what you paste. It cannot classify a substance or mixture, and it cannot tell whether an element is “applicable”; the classification decides that. Read the CLP Regulation on EUR-Lex (check the consolidated version) and the guides on CLP hazard information on the product page and missing hazard fields before publishing.
