Where the questions come from

The first rows follow the conditions of repair that the European Repair Information Form must specify under Article 4(4)(a) to (j) of Directive (EU) 2024/1799 on common rules promoting the repair of goods (the form itself is in Annex I), plus Article 4(2) and (3) on the form being free of charge and on diagnostic costs, and Article 4(5) on the 30 calendar days the conditions may not change. The product-category prompts come from the ten points of Annex II (goods with Union repairability requirements), which decide where the Article 5 obligation to repair and the Article 6 information duty apply. The other prompts come from Article 5(4) to (8) on spare parts, indicative prices, practices that impede repair and the consumer’s choice of repairer, from Article 16, which amends Directive (EU) 2019/771 on the legal guarantee (right to choose repair or replacement, a one-time 12-month extension of the liability period when goods are repaired), and from Articles 21 and 22 (national measures apply from 31 July 2026).

The page looks for words in what you paste. It cannot tell whether you are the manufacturer, the seller or the repairer, or whether a product is covered; the facts and your national law decide that. Annex II can be updated by delegated acts (Article 5(9)), so check for later changes. Read Directive (EU) 2024/1799 on EUR-Lex and the guides on EU right-to-repair proof and tracking repair requests.
