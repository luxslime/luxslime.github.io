App ownership evidence

Ownership evidence is the installed-app table (scopes + owner) — a staff user list is a different record

Staff Access Audit Guard (Shopify App Store) reviews installed app access, granted scopes, staff access records, and ownership evidence without reading customer or order data. It scores every installed app’s access risk, flags apps — including AI apps — that can read or write customer data, and tracks staff and collaborator access with 30/90-day review reminders. It does not revoke staff, uninstall apps, or attest that the store is secure. This page is not a security attestation.

Staff lists answer “who can log in”; ownership evidence answers “which app, which scopes, which owner”

Settings → Users shows people. Installed apps request scopes and have an owner on the install. Ownership evidence is the reviewable record of that app inventory — scopes and owner together — so a later audit can show what was granted and who owned the install. A screenshot of today’s staff list does not replace that table.

Shopify Admin can show app activity and data-privacy signals under Settings → Apps (see track app activity and permissions from Settings). That native view is useful context. A scored access table with owner and customer-data flags is the listing job on this page.

Install staff access audit

Listing sequence: audit, scopes + owner table, flags, drift

  1. Install and let the first audit run.
  2. Open the installed-app access table with scopes and owner — that is the ownership-evidence surface.
  3. Review apps flagged for customer-data read/write access, including AI apps, as the listing describes.
  4. Keep staff and collaborator access records alongside the app table when people access is also in scope.
  5. Re-run the audit after scope, owner, or cost changes.

Not the same as reminder cadence or “staff drift” as the primary question

  • 30/90-day review reminders — overdue people reviews versus platform collaborator expiry. See 30/90-day review reminders.
  • Staff access audit vs one-time user list — permission drift on people and apps as the hub narrative. See staff access audit.
  • Employee monitoring or customer-data export — not this listing. The job is store-level audit metadata and ownership evidence, without reading customer or order data.
