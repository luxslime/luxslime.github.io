Virgin wool meaning

In the EU, virgin wool is a protected name for wool that has not already been in a finished product

Direct answer. In the EU, “virgin wool” (or “fleece wool”) is a protected label name under Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 Article 8 and Annex III. It may be used only for wool fibre that has not previously been incorporated in a finished product, has not been spun or felted beyond what the product’s manufacture requires, and has not been damaged by treatment or use. Wool that has already been in a finished product does not meet Article 8(1), so that condition does not cover recycled wool. Quotes are from the consolidated English text CELEX 02011R1007-20180215 (15 February 2018), the latest consolidation the Publications Office listed on 13 October 2026. Article 7 and Article 8 carry no amendment marker in that text. This page is not legal advice.

Checklist

  • Use an Annex III name only when Article 8(1), or the Article 8(2) mixture conditions, are met.
  • “100 %”, “pure”, or “all” is Article 7(1), a different sentence from the virgin-wool names.
  • Merino and cashmere are Annex I entry 2 animal-hair names, not the Annex III virgin-wool names.
  • Put the fibre composition on the product page in the language of the market. The guides below already cover that line.

Article 8(1)

The article is titled “Fleece wool or virgin wool products.” Article 8(1): “A textile product may be labelled or marked by one of the names set out in Annex III provided it is composed exclusively of a wool fibre which has not previously been incorporated in a finished product, which has not been subjected to any spinning and/or felting processes other than those required in the manufacture of that product, and which has not been damaged by treatment or use.”

Article 8(2) and 8(3)

Article 8(2): “By way of derogation from paragraph 1, the names listed in Annex III may be used to describe wool contained in a textile fibre mixture if all the following conditions are met:”

  • (a) “all the wool contained in that mixture satisfies the requirements defined in paragraph 1;”
  • (b) “such wool accounts for not less than 25 % of the total weight of the mixture;”
  • (c) “in the case of a scribbled mixture, the wool is mixed with only one other fibre.”

“The full percentage composition of such mixture shall be given.”

Article 8(3): “The extraneous fibres in the products referred to in paragraphs 1 and 2, including wool products which have undergone a carding process, shall not exceed 0,3 % by weight, shall be justified as being technically unavoidable in good manufacturing practice and shall not be added as a matter of routine.”

Annex I entry 1, the fibre name “wool”

Annex I, table 1, number 1, name “wool”, description: “fibre from sheep's or lambs’ fleeces (Ovis aries) or a mixture of fibres from sheep's or lambs’ fleeces and the hairs of animals listed in number 2”.

“Pure new wool” and “new wool”

Article 7(1): “Only textile products exclusively composed of the same fibre may be labelled or marked as ‘100 %’, ‘pure’ or ‘all’.”

Annex III’s English line is “in English : ‘fleece wool’ or ‘virgin wool’”. The annex does not list an English name “pure new wool” or “new wool”. Other languages use their own names. German is “in German : ‘Schurwolle’”. Danish is “in Danish : ‘ren, ny uld’”. The Regulation text does not mention Woolmark. This page makes no claim about Woolmark licensing.

Annex III names in the consolidated text

The heading is “Names referred to in Article 8(1)”. The Croatian line is marked in the consolidation as inserted by Council Regulation (EU) No 517/2013 (marker M3). The other lines have no later amendment marker in this consolidation. No consolidation later than 15 February 2018 was listed on 13 October 2026.

Virgin wool, merino, and cashmere

Annex I number 2, name: “alpaca, llama, camel, cashmere, mohair, angora, vicuna, yak, guanaco, cashgora, beaver, otter, followed or not by the word ‘wool’ or ‘hair’”. Description: “hair of the following animals: alpaca, llama, camel, kashmir goat, angora goat, angora rabbit, vicuna, yak, guanaco, cashgora goat, beaver, otter”. Those are fibre names. They are not the Annex III virgin-wool names. The rest of the Annex I naming rule is EU textile fibre labels. This page does not compare warmth, quality, or durability.

The fibre line on a Shopify product page

  • 100 percent, blends, and other fibres

What this page is not

  • Not a quality, warmth, or durability ranking of wool, merino, or cashmere.
  • Not a Woolmark licence. The Regulation text does not mention Woolmark.
  • Not the original 2011 Official Journal alone. The names above are the 15 February 2018 consolidation, which adds the Croatian line.
