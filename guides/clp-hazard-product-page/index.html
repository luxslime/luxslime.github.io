A gallery photo is not a CLP label on the offer

Surface What the shopper gets before buy
On-pack CLP label Pictograms, signal word, H- and P-statements on the unit
Gallery / pack shot only Often unreadable; not structured statements
SDS in a footer After-the-fact download - not the offer itself
Product-page hazard block (this page) Same pictograms and statements as the profile, before purchase

Do not mix with Prop 65 (California chemical-name short form) or EU food allergen labelling - different templates.

Do CLP/GHS hazard pictograms have to appear on the product page?

For distance selling of hazardous substances and mixtures, shoppers need the CLP label elements before they buy — not only on the parcel. That set includes GHS pictograms, signal word, hazard and precautionary statements as they apply to the product. This page is not legal advice. Confirm Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008 as amended, including online-offer rules, not this paraphrase.

Is a photo of the bottle enough?

A pack shot can be unreadable on a phone and can omit statements the label requires. The job on this page is a structured hazard profile (pictograms + statements) shown on the product page, with missing fields reviewed before you publish. Do not treat a gallery image as the label.

Does an app classify the mixture or write the SDS?

No. Classification, labelling, and the safety data sheet are legal facts you already have (or your supplier has). A listing can store a hazard profile, sync it to a theme block via app-owned metafields, and keep an audit record of what you published. It does not decide the hazard class.

Is this the same as a California Prop 65 warning?

No. Prop 65 is a California safe-harbor warning with a listed chemical name. CLP is EU classification and labelling with GHS pictograms. Do not copy a Prop 65 short form onto a CLP product page, or the reverse.

Is this the same as EU food allergen labelling?

No. Food information (Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011) is a different pre-purchase job: Annex II allergens, nutrition, origin. CLP covers hazardous substances and mixtures. A food SKU and a detergent SKU do not share a label template.

What should I review before the hazard block goes live?

Missing pictograms, signal word, hazard statements, and precautionary statements relative to the profile you intend to show. Create the profile first; metafield sync to the storefront is a later step on the listing. Check the live product page after sync. Keep the audit record of what you published.
