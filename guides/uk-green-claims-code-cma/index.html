UK Green Claims Code

Green Claims Code (UK): the CMA’s six principles on GOV.UK

Direct answer. The Competition and Markets Authority’s Green Claims Code is guidance to help businesses comply with their existing obligations under consumer protection law. On the GOV.UK page the six principle headings are: claims must be truthful and accurate; claims must be clear and unambiguous; claims must not omit or hide important information; comparisons must be fair and meaningful; claims must be substantiated; and, in making the claim, you must consider the full life cycle of the product or service. The six headings, as published

The same GOV.UK page opens with this summary list, which is close to the headings but not word-for-word the same, and which puts the life-cycle principle before substantiation:

  • claims must be truthful and accurate
  • claims must be clear and unambiguous
  • claims must not omit or hide important relevant information
  • comparisons must be fair and meaningful
  • claims must consider the full life cycle of the product or service
  • claims must be substantiated

The chapter headings on that page, which this guide treats as the principle headings, are:

  • Claims must be truthful and accurate
  • Claims must be clear and unambiguous
  • Claims must not omit or hide important information
  • Comparisons must be fair and meaningful
  • Claims must be substantiated
  • In making the claim you must consider the full life cycle of the product or service.

GOV.UK says the principles help businesses understand how the CMA thinks the law applies. It also says the guidance is not legal advice, is not a substitute for the law, and does not replace the courts. The page says it is based on the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 and the Business Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008, and that if a business does not comply, the CMA and other bodies such as Trading Standards can bring court proceedings. The page says the guidance applies to businesses across the UK, including businesses in Northern Ireland, and to businesses based outside the UK selling to UK consumers.

The Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024

Separately from the Code page, the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 is on legislation.gov.uk. It is an Act to make provision relating to the protection of consumer rights, among other things. Commencement is not one date for the whole Act. Commencement No. 1 brings specified competition provisions into force on 1 January 2025. Commencement No. 2 brings Part 3 (enforcement of consumer protection law), and Chapter 1 of Part 4 (protection from unfair trading) except sections 232, 234, and 235, into force on 6 April 2025 to the extent they were not already in force, and brings Chapter 3 of Part 4 (consumer savings schemes) into force on 1 January 2026 to that same extent. Confirm the live commencement instruments before you treat a CMA power as in force. The Green Claims Code page quoted above still describes court proceedings and the 2008 regulations. This guide does not merge that page and the 2024 Act into one rule.

This is not the EU generic-claim ban

Directive (EU) 2024/825 is a different instrument. What it does to generic environmental wording on product copy is covered on EU generic environmental claims. This UK page does not restate that directive.

Checklist before you publish a green claim

  • Read the six headings on the GOV.UK page, including the places where the summary list and the chapter headings use slightly different words.
  • Treat the Code as CMA guidance on existing consumer law, not as a new labelling regulation by itself.
  • Check legislation.gov.uk for which parts of the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 are in force. Do not take one commencement date as the start of the whole Act.
  • If the copy is for the EU, use the linked EU guide rather than this Code page.
  • Keep the evidence the substantiation heading points you to. This page does not decide that a sentence is lawful.

What this page is not

  • Not legal advice, and not the CMA’s guidance itself. The GOV.UK page is the source.
  • Not a statement that every section of the 2024 Act is in force.
  • Not the EU ban on generic green claims. That URL is EU generic environmental claims.
