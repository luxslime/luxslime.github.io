CLP hazard statements

Annex III Part 1 prints the hazard-statement wording. Article 21 puts it on the label

Direct answer. In the consolidated text of Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008, CELEX 02008R1272-20260701 (EN, 01.07.2026; reference line 02008R1272 — EN — 01.07.2026 — 031.001), Article 21 is titled “Hazard statements.” Article 21(1) says the label shall include the relevant hazard statements in accordance with the classification. Article 21(2) says the statements relevant for each classification are set out in the tables of label elements in Parts 2 to 5 of Annex I. Article 21(4) says: “The hazard statements shall be worded in accordance with Annex III.” Annex III is titled “LIST OF HAZARD STATEMENTS, SUPPLEMENTAL HAZARD INFORMATION AND SUPPLEMENTAL LABEL ELEMENTS.” Part 1 is hazard statements. The tables below copy the English wording that consolidation prints in Table 1.1 (physical hazards), Table 1.2 (health hazards), and Table 1.3 (environmental hazards), including the EUH codes that Part 1 itself prints. This page is not legal advice.

CLPLens (Shopify App Store) records CLP hazard display data, syncs app-owned product metafields, and shows storefront hazard information with audit records. It does not classify the substance or mixture. Supplemental EUH statements in Annex II are a separate list.

Checklist before you copy a code onto a label

  • Open Annex III Part 1 on the 1 July 2026 consolidated text. The wording below is that English text.
  • Article 21(2) assigns the statement from the Annex I label-element tables for the classification. Annex III is the wording, not a substitute for classification.
  • Combined statements in the tables may be used. The precedence rules at the start of Part 1 are quoted below.
  • EUH statements that Annex II still prints, including EUH208 and EUH210, are the EUH list. This page does not repeat that set.
  • Precautionary statements are Annex IV. The P-code list is CLP precautionary statements. The label-element checklist is CLP label elements. This page does not list P-codes.

What Part 1 says about applying the statements

The opening of Annex III Part 1 says the hazard statements shall be applied in accordance with Parts 2, 3, 4 and 5 of Annex I. It says that, in selecting the hazard statements in accordance with Articles 21 and 27, suppliers may use the combined hazard statements provided for in the annex. In accordance with Article 27, the following principles of precedence may apply to labelling, as that text prints them:

  • if the hazard statement H410 ‘Very toxic to aquatic life with long lasting effects’ is assigned, the statement H400 ‘Very toxic to aquatic life’ may be omitted;
  • if the statement H314 ‘Causes severe skin burns and eye damage’ is assigned, the statement H318 ‘Causes serious eye damage’ may be omitted;
  • if the hazard statement EUH441 ‘Strongly accumulates in the environment and living organisms including in humans’ is assigned, the statement EUH440 ‘Accumulates in the environment and living organisms including in humans’ may be omitted;
  • if the hazard statement EUH451 ‘Can cause very long-lasting and diffuse contamination of water resources’ is assigned, the statement EUH450 ‘Can cause long-lasting and diffuse contamination of water resources’ may be omitted.

The same part says that, in order to indicate the route of administration or exposure, the combined hazard statements in Table 1.2 may be used. Placeholders in angle brackets are printed as the consolidation prints them, including the space before the closing bracket in H360.

EUH statements and precautionary statements

Annex II is the set of supplemental EUH statements, including the sentences Article 25 sends to the label. That set, as the same consolidation prints it, is CLP EUH statements. The EUH codes in the tables above are the ones Annex III Part 1 itself prints inside Tables 1.2 and 1.3. They are not a second copy of the Annex II list.

Precautionary statements are in Annex IV, titled “LIST OF PRECAUTIONARY STATEMENTS.” Article 22 says the label shall include the relevant precautionary statements, selected from the tables in Parts 2 to 5 of Annex I and worded in accordance with Part 2 of Annex IV. The English wording in Part 1 is the precautionary-statement list. Which label elements to look for is the label-elements checklist. This page does not list P-codes. Signal words are Article 20. Pictogram size is Annex I section 1.2.1.

Advertisement and distance sales

Article 48 is titled “Advertisement.” Article 48(1) says any advertisement for a substance classified as hazardous shall indicate, as applicable, the hazard pictograms, signal words, hazard statements and supplemental EUH statements set out in Annex II, and that any advertisement for such a substance for sale to the general public shall in addition state: ‘Always follow the information on the product label.’. Article 48(2) says the same indication for a mixture classified as hazardous or covered by Article 25(6), and the same additional sentence for sale to the general public. Article 48(4) says that, by way of derogation from paragraphs 1 and 2, the hazard pictograms and signal words may be omitted where the advertisement is non-visual.

Article 48a is titled “Distance sales offers.” It says: “When substances or mixtures are placed on the market through distance sales, the offer shall clearly and visibly indicate the label elements referred to in Article 17.” What that means on a product page is CLP hazard on the product page. This page does not repeat that job.

What this page is not

  • Not a classification. Annex III is the wording after the classification is known.
  • Not the Annex II EUH set. That URL is CLP EUH statements.
  • Not a P-statement list. Precautionary statements are Annex IV, on the label-elements checklist.
  • Not the product-page display. Article 48a points to Article 17. That reading is CLP hazard on the product page.
  • Not a substitute for the live consolidated text. Confirm CELEX 02008R1272-20260701 before you print a sentence.
