CRA duty records: a product file before an Article 14 incident

CRALedger Duty Records (Shopify App Store) is a Shopify app that tracks product roles, checklists and open record gaps, Article 14 incident deadlines, and encrypted ENISA SRP draft packs, with exportable record packs and chronological audit evidence. It does not submit the ENISA notification or determine your legal role.

Keep the role, checklist, open gaps, and Article 14 clocks before you must report. You still file on ENISA. The app does not submit the notification. Not legal advice.

Does the CRA apply to my store, and in which role?

The Cyber Resilience Act applies to products with digital elements — hardware or software whose intended purpose or reasonably foreseeable use includes a direct or indirect connection to a device or network — when they are made available on the EU market. Some sectors are excluded, for example medical devices and motor vehicles; this page does not list every exclusion. Not legal advice.

CRA roles for a connected-product seller (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847)
Role When it applies, and what you check
Manufacturer You design or make the product, or you sell it under your own name or trademark. You carry the full duties, including Article 14 reporting.
Importer You place a non-EU manufacturer’s product on the EU market. You check that the manufacturer did conformity assessment, documentation and CE marking, and that the support period is stated.
Distributor You resell. You check CE marking and documentation, and act if you know of a vulnerability.

An importer or distributor who sells under its own name or trademark, or substantially modifies the product, takes on manufacturer duties (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, Art. 21).

From 11 September 2026, manufacturers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents via ENISA’s Single Reporting Platform (early warning within 24 hours, Art. 14). Most other obligations apply from 11 December 2027 (Art. 71).

What to do now:

  • Decide your role per product.
  • Keep the product file (role, support period, contact point, open gaps).
  • Know who files on ENISA and how fast.

CRALedger keeps that duty record and the Article 14 clocks. You still file on ENISA, and the app does not submit notifications or decide your role.

Do I have to show the support period on the product page?

Yes. Manufacturers must ensure the end date of the support period, “including at least the month and the year, is clearly and understandably specified at the time of purchase in an easily accessible manner and, where applicable, on the product with digital elements, its packaging or by digital means” (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, Art. 13(19)). For an online shop, the practical place is the product page before Add to cart, for example “Security updates until 12/2031”.

The support period reflects the time the product is expected to be in use and is at least five years, unless the product is expected to be in use for less than five years (Art. 13(8)).

Before making the product available on the market, distributors must verify that the manufacturer and the importer complied with Art. 13(19), among other obligations (Art. 20(2)(b)). A reseller should check that the date is supplied and shown, and should not invent one.

These product obligations apply from 11 December 2027 (Art. 71(2)). Article 14 reporting already applies from 11 September 2026.

Native Shopify way to display it: a product metafield (for example a date or text “Security updates until”) shown with the theme’s metafield / text block near the price. No app is required to display it.

When do Cyber Resilience Act Article 14 reporting duties apply?

Manufacturers of products with digital elements must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents that affect product security from 11 September 2026. Notifications go through ENISA’s Single Reporting Platform to ENISA and the coordinating CSIRT. The CRA’s main cybersecurity requirements apply later, from 11 December 2027. This page is not legal advice. Confirm Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 and ENISA’s SRP pages.

Does keeping duty records mean I filed an ENISA notification?

No. Filing is a separate act on the Single Reporting Platform. A duty-records app can track roles, checklists, open gaps, Article 14 deadlines, and draft encrypted SRP packs. It does not submit the notification, obtain a case number, or replace ENISA’s platform.

What belongs in the product duty file before an incident?

Product role (manufacturer vs other actors), the checklist of duties that role carries, open record gaps, and the Article 14 clocks you will need if you become aware of active exploitation or a severe incident (early warning, follow-up, final report). Chronological audit evidence is how you retrieve that later. Confirm the live articles and Commission guidance, not this page.

Do Article 14 duties apply only after 11 December 2027?

No. Reporting is the early slice. Essential cybersecurity requirements, CE marking, and the rest of the CRA’s main obligations apply from 11 December 2027. Do not wait until 2027 to invent a product file if you already place products with digital elements on the Union market.

Is this the same as an EUDR lot registry or a staff access audit?

No. EUDR stores supplier-lot DDS reference numbers for deforestation-relevant commodities. An access audit scores staff and installed-app scopes. CRA duty records are product-with-digital-elements obligations and incident/vulnerability reporting. Do not mix those files.

Does an app make the catalog CRA compliant?

No. Whether you are a manufacturer, whether a SKU is a product with digital elements, and whether a notification was validly submitted are legal facts. A listing can store roles, gaps, deadlines, and draft packs. It does not file, decide your role, or guarantee compliance. Not legal advice.
