EU textile fibre composition must use Annex I names, not trade names

Job: put a fibre composition line on the product page that uses official Annex I names (EN, DE, FR) and percentages — not “Lycra”, “Tencel”, or other trade names. Recording a blend is easy; using accepted names is the gap.

This page is the textile-label job, not a listing mirror and not legal advice. Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 is the fibre-name catalogue. French online listings also have place-of-manufacture rules. Confirm the live texts before you ship copy.

Field Product-page consequence

Do EU textile fibre labels have to use Annex I names?

Yes when you are using the fibre names that Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 allows on a textile product. Annex I is the catalogue of fibre names. Composition must be indicated; distance selling still has to make that composition available before purchase. This page is not legal advice. Confirm Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011, not this page.

Can I put Lycra or Tencel on the fibre composition line?

Those are trade names, not Annex I fibre names. Elastane / viscose (or the official name that actually applies) belong on the composition line. A brand name can appear elsewhere if you still state the lawful fibre name and percentage. Flag trade names instead of copying them into the composition field.

Do French online listings need places of manufacture on the product page?

French rules for certain textile and footwear listings require the geographic origin of main manufacturing operations on the product page. The listing this site uses treats that place-of-make field as in force since 10 July 2026. Confirm the live French text, not this page.

What changes on 1 October 2026 for environmental display?

From 1 October 2026, merchants may also record a displayed environmental impact score with cited sources, as the listing describes that optional record. That is not a substitute for Annex I fibre names. Confirm the live French affichage environnemental measures, not this page.

Does validating fibre names make the listing “textile-label compliant”?

No. Fibre-name checks, place-of-make fields, EPR registration records, and an audit trail are records you keep. They do not decide whether the garment is in scope, whether percentages are accurate, or whether a French or Dutch EPR number is valid. Not legal advice and not a compliance certification.

Is this the same as EU generic green claims on product copy?

No. Annex I fibre names are composition labelling. “Eco-friendly cotton” is a generic environmental claim job. Do not treat a fibre-name pass as a green-claim pass.

Where do I validate Annex I fibre names for the product page?

Check every fibre against the official Annex I catalogue in EN, DE, and FR, flag trade names, and record French places of manufacture where that rule applies.
