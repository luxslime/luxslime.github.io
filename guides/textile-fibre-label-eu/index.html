EU textile fibre composition label

EU textile fibre composition must use Annex I names, not trade names

FibreLedger Textile Labels (Shopify App Store) is a Shopify app that checks every fibre name against the official Annex I catalogue of Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 in EN, DE and FR, flags trade names such as Lycra or Tencel that are not lawful fibre names, and records French places of manufacture for online textile listings.

Job: put a fibre composition line on the product page that uses official Annex I names (EN, DE, FR) and percentages - not “Lycra”, “Tencel”, or other trade names.

Where must fibre composition appear on a Shopify product page?

  1. The rule: Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 Art. 16(1) requires the fibre composition to be available to the consumer before purchase, including when buying online. The Commission FAQ on Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011, Q6.9, says the composition “needs to be present together with other product-related information, notably price, whenever this information enables performing a sales transaction, including online purchases.” Q6.3: the label must be clearly visible to the consumer before purchase. Put the composition on the product page near the price / Add to cart. A care-label PDF, a hidden tab after checkout, or the order email alone is not enough. This page is not legal advice.
  2. Format: use Annex I names with percentages, in the language(s) of the market (for example “95% cotton, 5% elastane”, not “Lycra”). See the wrong/right contrast later on this page.
  3. Native Shopify option: create a product metafield (for example custom.fibre_composition) and show it with the theme’s metafield / text block placed in the product info section near the price. This works without an app. The app helps with validation, not display rights.
  4. When the app path fits: FibreLedger Textile Labels validates fibre names against Annex I (EN, DE, FR), flags trade names such as Lycra or Tencel, and records French places of manufacture where that rule applies. It does not certify compliance. This page is not legal advice.

Do EU textile fibre labels have to use Annex I names?

Yes when you are using the fibre names that Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 allows on a textile product. Annex I is the catalogue of fibre names. Composition must be indicated; distance selling still has to make that composition available before purchase. This page is not legal advice. Confirm Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011, not this page.

Can I put Lycra or Tencel on the fibre composition line?

The composition line must use the Annex I name (elastane, lyocell). A trade mark may sit immediately before or after it — for example “95% cotton, 5% elastane (LYCRA®)” or “100% lyocell (TENCEL™)” — but it cannot replace the Annex I name. Other marketing text goes separately. See Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 Art. 16(2). Labelling must be in the official language(s) of the Member State where the product is made available, unless that state provides otherwise (Art. 16(3)). That language job is on EU textile fibre composition language of the market. This page is not legal advice.

Do French online listings need places of manufacture on the product page?

Yes for the online-display layer, and that job is not Annex I fibre names. Two French instruments apply. The dedicated page is France places of manufacture vs EU fibre names (Annex I).

This page stays on Annex I fibre names. FibreLedger records French places of manufacture as a separate listing job. Not legal advice.

What changes on 1 October 2026 for environmental display?

From 1 October 2026, merchants may also record a displayed environmental impact score with cited sources, as the listing describes that optional record. That is not a substitute for Annex I fibre names. Confirm the live French affichage environnemental measures, not this page.

Does validating fibre names make the listing “textile-label compliant”?

No. Fibre-name checks, place-of-make fields, EPR registration records, and an audit trail are records you keep. They do not decide whether the garment is in scope, whether percentages are accurate, or whether a French or Dutch EPR number is valid. Not legal advice and not a compliance certification.

Is this the same as EU generic green claims on product copy?

No. Annex I fibre names are composition labelling. “Eco-friendly cotton” is a generic environmental claim job. Do not treat a fibre-name pass as a green-claim pass.
