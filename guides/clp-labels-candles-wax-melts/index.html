CLP labels for candles and wax melts

CLP labels for candles and wax melts: classify the mixture, then apply Art. 17

Direct answer. Official EU guidance treats candles, including wax melts, as chemical mixtures under REACH and CLP. If that mixture is classified as hazardous under Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008 (for example because of an added fragrance), the pack must carry the Article 17 label elements. Classification is formulation-specific; this page does not classify any SKU and is not legal advice. For Great Britain, the UK HSE CLP labelling pages are the GB authority pages to read alongside GB CLP.

Mixtures first, then the Art. 17 set

The Publications Office note Complying with CLP when making or importing candles states that candle products are chemical mixtures under REACH and CLP, and that a candle classified as hazardous (due to an added fragrance, for example) must meet CLP before it is placed on the EU market. That note includes wax melts with candles. It does not say every unscented wax is hazardous, and this page does not invent that either.

If the classified mixture is in packaging, Article 17 of CLP lists the on-pack elements: supplier name, address and telephone number; nominal quantity for general-public packages (unless specified elsewhere on the package); product identifiers; hazard pictograms; a signal word; hazard statements; precautionary statements; and supplemental information where applicable. Walk the full set on CLP Art. 17 label elements. Read the live article on EUR-Lex.

Which of those items apply follows from the classification and from the rest of CLP, not from a product type name such as “candle” or “wax melt.” A reading aid such as the CLP label element checker can only flag words and codes in pasted text; it cannot classify the mixture.

Great Britain: HSE and GB CLP

For substances and mixtures placed on the GB market, HSE is the GB CLP agency. HSE states that classification is the supplier’s duty, that a chemical is a substance or a mixture, and that mixture classification is generally based on what is known of the constituent substances. See HSE: how classification works and HSE: duties of a GB-based manufacturer. The HSE labelling and packaging hub covers the hazard label, statements, signal words, and pictograms. Confirm GB CLP as it stands; this page does not equate every EU amendment with GB law.
