Shopify missing hasMerchantReturnPolicy / shippingDetails

The Search Console warning is non-critical — set returns and shipping once (Search Console, Merchant Center, or Organization markup) before hand-editing every offer

Agentic Catalog by momiji (Shopify App Store) reviews policy, shipping, tax, domain, and product data in one readiness score, and audits Product JSON-LD completeness on live product pages. The Search Console warning “Missing field ‘hasMerchantReturnPolicy’” / “Missing field ‘shippingDetails’ (in ‘offers’)” flags recommended merchant-listing properties on Offer — not a required field, and not an error that removes the product from Search. Set a store-wide return and shipping policy once, then re-score. The app audits and scores those live surfaces; you apply the policy change yourself.

Job: give Google one store-wide returns and shipping policy (Search Console, Merchant Center, or organization-level markup) instead of hand-editing every product offer unless a product truly has a non-standard policy.

What the warning means

Google’s merchant listing structured data documentation splits Offer properties into required and recommended. Required properties (for merchant-listing eligibility) include the active offer price / priceSpecification.price and the matching currency. hasMerchantReturnPolicy and shippingDetails sit under Recommended properties on Offer. Google says you must include the required properties for the content to be eligible as a rich result, and you can include the recommended properties to add more information.

The same merchant-listing page tells you to validate with the Rich Results Test and “fix any critical errors,” then: “Consider also fixing any non-critical issues that may be flagged in the tool, as they can help improve the quality of your structured data (however, this isn't necessary to be eligible for rich results).” That is the class this Search Console warning belongs to. It is a recommended enhancement for merchant listings, not a required-field failure that takes the product out of Search.

Google also recommends a global policy under Organization rather than repeating return and shipping markup on every offer. Offer-level properties are for overrides, or for stores that have no standard policy.

Three ways to give Google the policy

Google’s November 2025 Search Central post More ways to share your shipping and returns policies with Google is the store-wide path most product-page Liquid edits skip. You can tell Google about shipping and returns in two distinct ways without touching every offer: configure them directly in Search Console, or use organization-level structured data. Merchant Center remains the path for merchants who already keep delivery and return settings there.

  1. Search Console shipping and returns settings. Google expanded the “Shipping and returns” feature in Search Console so it is available to websites identified as online merchants, including merchants without a Merchant Center account. That is a UI path: set the policy once. The blog states that “settings configured in Search Console will take precedence over structured data on your site.” The return-policy and shipping-policy docs say you can configure account-level policies in Search Console, “which get automatically added to Merchant Center.”
  2. Merchant Center (existing Google & YouTube channel / Merchant Center account). The return-policy and shipping-policy docs say that if you have a Google Merchant Center account and keeping markup current is hard, configure return policies and delivery settings in Merchant Center. Merchant listing’s “combining multiple” list also places product-level feeds submitted in Merchant Center and Content API for Shopping settings above on-page markup.
  3. Organization-level markup on the refund or shipping policy page. Nest hasMerchantReturnPolicy and hasShippingService under an OnlineStore (a subtype of Organization). Google recommends placing return information on a single page that describes the return policy, and shipping structured data on the page that describes the shipping policy — not on every product URL. Test that URL in the Rich Results Test.

Precedence, as Google states it (strongest to weakest):

  • Return-policy doc: Content API for Shopping (return settings), then settings in Merchant Center or Search Console, then product-level merchant listing markup, then organization-level markup. “If you provide both return policy markup on your site and return policy settings in Search Console, Google will only use the information provided in Search Console.”
  • Shipping-policy doc: settings in Merchant Center or Search Console, then product-level merchant listing markup, then organization-level markup. Same Search Console-over-markup example.
  • Merchant-listing “combining multiple” list: product-level feeds submitted in Merchant Center, then Content API for Shopping settings, then settings in Merchant Center or Search Console, then product-level merchant listing markup, then organization-level markup.
  • Product-level overrides organization-level. The shipping blog: “Shipping policies specified for individual products will still take priority over this general, organization-level policy for those specific items.” The merchant-listing return section: “If you provide both organization-level and product-level return policy markup, Google defaults to the product-level return policy.”

A short organization-level shape (placeholders only; put this on your policy page, not as a live block on this guide):

{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "OnlineStore",
  "name": "Example Store",
  "url": "https://www.example.com",
  "hasMerchantReturnPolicy": {
    "@type": "MerchantReturnPolicy",
    "applicableCountry": ["XX"],
    "returnPolicyCountry": "XX",
    "returnPolicyCategory": "https://schema.org/MerchantReturnFiniteReturnWindow",
    "merchantReturnDays": 30,
    "returnMethod": "https://schema.org/ReturnByMail",
    "returnFees": "https://schema.org/FreeReturn",
    "refundType": "https://schema.org/FullRefund"
  },
  "hasShippingService": {
    "@type": "ShippingService",
    "name": "Standard shipping",
    "fulfillmentType": "FulfillmentTypeDelivery"
  }
}

Copy the property names from Google’s return policy, shipping policy, and Organization pages. Replace the placeholders with your live policy. The legal refund-policy wording on the storefront is still the merchant’s job.

When offer-level markup is still right

Use offer-level hasMerchantReturnPolicy and shippingDetails only when a product has a non-standard policy, or when you have no store-wide policy. Google says product-level return and shipping markup supports a subset of the organization-level properties. On Offer, the merchant-listing subset for returns includes required applicableCountry and returnPolicyCategory (plus merchantReturnDays when the window is finite), and recommended returnMethod, returnFees, and returnShippingFeesAmount. For shipping, if you add shippingDetails, Google’s OfferShippingDetails required set for the shipping-details enhancement is deliveryTime, shippingDestination, and shippingRate.

Google’s own reference pattern points an offer at the organization policy with @id instead of repeating the full object:

{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Offer",
  "hasMerchantReturnPolicy": {
    "@id": "https://www.example.com/policies/refund-policy#returns"
  },
  "shippingDetails": {
    "@type": "OfferShippingDetails",
    "hasShippingService": {
      "@id": "https://www.example.com/policies/shipping-policy#shipping"
    }
  }
}

Hand-editing every Shopify product offer for a store-wide 30-day return window is the expensive path. Set the organization policy (or Search Console / Merchant Center settings) first. Only then add offer-level markup for the products that truly differ.

Where Shopify themes print Product JSON-LD

Open a live product page. Use View source (or equivalent). Search for application/ld+json. That is the source of truth for this theme, this product URL. Do not assume every Shopify theme emits the same Product graph, and do not assume the graph includes hasMerchantReturnPolicy or shippingDetails.

On Shopify’s Dawn theme, Product JSON-LD is printed from sections/main-product.liquid as:

<script type="application/ld+json">
  {{ product | structured_data }}
</script>

That uses Shopify’s Liquid structured_data filter. Other themes may hand-build a Product object, skip JSON-LD, or inject a second block. Check the live PDP. Completing return or shipping settings in Search Console does not, by itself, add those keys to the theme’s Product JSON-LD — and it does not need to, because Search Console / Merchant Center settings outrank on-page markup when both exist.

Not the same as GTIN, Open Graph, robots.txt, or llms.txt

  • GTIN / first-barcode Admin fields are product identifiers. That path is Shopify GTIN / barcode for AI shopping agents.
  • Open Graph share tags are a different live PDP surface from Product JSON-LD. That path is Open Graph is not Product JSON-LD.
  • robots.txt allow vs block for GPTBot, ClaudeBot, or PerplexityBot is crawler policy. That path is GPTBot / ClaudeBot / PerplexityBot allow vs block.
  • agents.md / llms.txt customization is a theme-template context file. That path is customize Shopify agents.md and llms.txt.

None of those jobs writes a return or shipping policy for Google. The legal refund-policy and shipping-policy content on the storefront is the merchant’s. This page is about how Google is told that policy exists — not a substitute for counsel, and not a rewrite of the policy text.
