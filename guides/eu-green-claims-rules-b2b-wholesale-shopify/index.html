EmpCo and B2B stores

Wholesale only on Shopify: do the EU green-claims rules apply to B2B pages?

Direct answer. The EU’s 2024 green-claims changes (Directive (EU) 2024/825) amend the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, which applies to business-to-consumer practices, so a store that sells only to other businesses is outside its Annex I bans. A store that also sells to consumers has to meet the rules on every page a consumer can reach, and misleading claims aimed at other traders can still fall under the Misleading Advertising Directive (2006/114/EC).

Which directive covers whom

The Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (2005/29/EC) says in Article 3(1) that it applies to “unfair business-to-consumer commercial practices”. Directive (EU) 2024/825 adds the new green-claims rules to it, including the Annex I list of practices that are always unfair from 27 September 2026.

The Misleading Advertising Directive (2006/114/EC) has a different purpose. Article 1 says it is to “protect traders against misleading advertising”, and Article 3 lists the features of goods a court looks at, including their composition and method of manufacture. It does not carry the 2024 Annex I bans.

Sources: Directive 2005/29/EC, Directive 2006/114/EC, Directive (EU) 2024/825 on EUR-Lex.

A wholesale tier does not make a store B2B only

The consumer rules attach to the practice, not to the store label. A public product page that a shopper can reach is a consumer-facing page even if you built it for trade buyers. A page behind a trade login that only business customers can open is a different case, and the audience test is yours to document.

National law can go beyond the directive. Check how the Member States you sell into transposed both directives before you rely on a B2B exemption.

Sort your pages by audience

  1. List every page that carries environmental wording: product pages, collections, About, announcement bar, wholesale landing pages, line sheets and PDFs you link from the store.
  2. Mark each page as consumer-reachable or trade-only. If a trade page is public, treat it as consumer-reachable.
  3. For consumer-reachable pages, run the generic-claim, offset and label checks on the wording.
  4. For trade-only pages, keep any environmental statement supportable anyway, because misleading advertising between traders is still regulated.
  5. Write down the date and the audience call for each page.
