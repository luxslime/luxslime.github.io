Prop 65 warning type size

The 6-point Prop 65 type-size sentence is the short-form rule on the product label

Direct answer. In OEHHA’s final regulatory text for 27 CCR § 25602(a)(4), a short-form warning on the product label must use this type size: “The entire warning must be in a type size no smaller than the largest type size used for other consumer information on the product. In no case shall the warning appear in a type size smaller than 6-point type.” Section 25601(c) in that same text is the prominent-display rule. It does not itself state a point size. Sections 25602(b) and 25602(c), the internet and catalog methods, do not add a point-size sentence. An internet warning still has to be on the product display page, behind a clearly marked hyperlink, or otherwise prominently displayed to the purchaser prior to completing the purchase. Checklist before you treat a point size as required

  • Read OEHHA’s final regulatory text and the adoption notice, then the live 27 CCR if either has moved.
  • Use the 6-point sentence only where § 25602(a)(4) places it: a short-form warning on the product label.
  • For a label method that is not that short-form method, do not copy the 6-point sentence across. See OEHHA.
  • For an internet or catalog warning, read § 25602(b) and (c). Those subsections do not state a point size.
  • The warning sentence itself, including the chemical name, is the short-form guide and the warning examples. This page does not repeat it.

On the product: conspicuousness, then the short-form point size

Section 25601(c) of the final regulatory text requires consumer product exposure warnings to be prominently displayed on a label, labeling, or sign, and displayed with such conspicuousness, as compared with other words, statements, designs, or devices, as to render the warning likely to be seen, read, and understood by an ordinary individual under customary conditions of purchase or use. That subsection does not state “6-point” or any other point size.

The point-size sentences sit in § 25602(a)(4), the short-form warning on the label. That method also says the warning is provided on a product in a type size that complies with § 25601(c), and then states the two sentences quoted above. Section 25602(a)(3), a warning on the label that complies with § 25603(a), does not repeat those point-size sentences. If you need a point size for a long-form label and the section in front of you does not state one, see OEHHA.

Section 25603(a)(1) sizes the warning symbol, not the paragraph: the symbol shall be placed to the left of the text of the warning, in a size no smaller than the height of the word “WARNING”. That is a symbol-height rule. It is not a second point size for the warning text.

Internet and catalog: before purchase, with no extra point size in these subsections

Section 25602(b) is the internet method. As the final text sets it out, the warning uses one or more of these methods: a warning on the product display page; a clearly marked hyperlink using the word “WARNING” or the words “CA WARNING” or “CALIFORNIA WARNING” on the product display page; or an otherwise prominently displayed warning provided to the purchaser prior to completing the purchase. The same subsection says a warning is not prominently displayed if the purchaser must search for it in the general content of the website. If the label uses short-form content under § 25602(a)(4), the website may use that same content. None of those internet sentences states a point size.

Section 25602(c) says that for catalog purchases, a warning that complies with the content requirements of § 25603 must also be provided in the catalog in a manner that clearly associates it with the item being purchased. If a short-form warning is on the label under § 25602(a)(4), the catalog may use the same content. That subsection does not state a point size either.
