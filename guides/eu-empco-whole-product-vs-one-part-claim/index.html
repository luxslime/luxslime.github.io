EmpCo whole-product claims

“Made with recycled materials” when only the packaging is: the EU whole-product claim rule

Direct answer. From 27 September 2026, making an environmental claim about the entire product or business when it concerns only one aspect or activity is banned in all circumstances (Annex I, point 4b of the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive), and the directive’s own example is a product marketed as “made with recycled material” when only the packaging is. Name the part the claim covers in the same line, for instance “packaging made from recycled paper”, or drop the claim.

Green Claims Guard (Shopify App Store) flags green-claim wording and names the exact provision it cites. Whole-product wording such as “made with recycled materials” is exactly the kind of line to scan and review. The app does not check what your product is made of, and this page is not legal advice.

What the rule says

Annex I point 4b, added by Directive (EU) 2024/825, covers making an environmental claim about the entire product or the trader’s entire business when it concerns only a certain aspect of the product or a specific activity of the business. The recitals give two examples: “made with recycled material” when only the packaging is recycled, and implying only renewable energy is used when several facilities still use fossil fuels.

Source: Directive (EU) 2024/825 on EUR-Lex, Annex, point (2) (new points 4a–4c), and recital 11.

Lines on a Shopify store that blur the part

  • “Made with recycled materials” in a product title or short description when the recycled part is the box or mailer.
  • “Eco packaging” placed beside the product name so it reads as a product claim.
  • “Powered by renewable energy” on a brand page when only some sites use it.
  • A badge or tag on a collection that applies to one item in it.

A rewrite pattern: part, claim, scope

State the part, the specific claim and its scope in the same line.

  • Instead of “made with recycled materials”: “mailer box made from recycled paper; the product itself is not made from recycled material”, if that is true.
  • Instead of “runs on renewable energy”: the sites or processes where it is true, in the same sentence.

The recitals add that a specific claim given in clear and prominent terms on the same medium is not a generic claim, and that claims about the whole business remain possible when accurate, verifiable and not overstated.

A short review run

  1. Scan your product copy with Green Claims Guard.
  2. For each flag, read the provision it cites and ask which part of the product or business the line is really about.
  3. Rewrite the line to name that part, or remove it.
  4. If a percentage is involved, check recycled-content % claims.
  5. Re-scan after the edit so the live copy matches what you approved.

Not the same as the neighbouring rules

  • Unspecified words such as “eco-friendly”: generic claims ban.
  • “Recyclable”: recyclable claims.
  • Offset-based neutrality: carbon-neutral claims.
