Prop 65 short-form warning

Prop 65 short-form warning content, and when §25602 lets a label use it

Direct answer. A short-form consumer-product warning is one safe-harbor method in 27 CCR § 25602(a)(4): a warning on the label that meets the content in § 25603(b) and the type-size rules written in that method. OEHHA’s final regulatory text for § 25603(b) includes the warning symbol, the word “WARNING:” or the words “CA WARNING:” or “CALIFORNIA WARNING:”, and one of the short sentences that names a chemical. If the label uses that short-form content, § 25602(b) and (c) say the website or catalog may use the same content. OEHHA’s adoption notice states that the Office of Administrative Law approved the rulemaking on 26 November 2024 and that the effective date is 1 January 2025. The same final text includes earlier short-form lines for a product manufactured and labeled prior to 1 January 2028. Confirm the live Code of Regulations if either date or sentence has moved. This page quotes structure only. It is not legal advice.

Checklist: short-form method before you paste a sentence

  • Read live 27 CCR §§ 25602 and 25603, plus OEHHA’s adoption notice, not a stored copy of this page.
  • Use short-form on a label only as § 25602(a)(4) describes it: content that meets § 25603(b), in a type size no smaller than the largest type used for other consumer information on the product, and not smaller than 6-point type, as that method states.
  • Name a chemical in the short sentence. The brackets in the regulation are placeholders, not a chemical this page chooses.
  • For an internet or catalog sale, confirm whether § 25602 lets that surface reuse the label’s short-form content.
  • If the product was manufactured and labeled before the date the final text states, check the earlier short-form lines in that text before you drop the chemical name.
  • Food short-form content is § 25607.2, not the consumer-product lines in § 25603(b). Walk that job on Prop 65 food short-form on Shopify.

When the short-form method is the one § 25602 describes

Section 25602(a) lists methods of transmission. One of them, (a)(4), is a short-form warning on the label that complies with § 25603(b). The final regulatory text also says the entire warning must be in a type size no smaller than the largest type size used for other consumer information on the product, and in no case smaller than 6-point type. This page does not add a package-size cutoff the section does not state.

For internet purchases, § 25602(b) says a warning may be on the product display page, or behind a clearly marked hyperlink using the word “WARNING” or the words “CA WARNING” or “CALIFORNIA WARNING”, or otherwise prominently displayed before the purchase is completed. The same subsection says that if the warning uses short-form label content under § 25602(a)(4), the website may use that same content. Catalog purchases in § 25602(c) have the matching catalog sentence. The longer method list is on Prop 65 warning examples.

The final text of § 25602(b)(2) also describes a 60-calendar-day period, for internet purchases made before 1 January 2028, before a retail seller is responsible for posting an updated short-form warning after notice. Read that subsection before you treat the 60 days as covering every seller or every date.

Short-form content structure (§ 25603(b))

As written in OEHHA’s final regulatory text, a short-form warning on the label under § 25602(a)(4) includes:

  • The symbol required for the long-form warning: a black exclamation point in a yellow equilateral triangle with a bold black outline (black and white where the label is not printed in yellow), to the left of the text.
  • The word “WARNING:” or the words “CA WARNING:” or “CALIFORNIA WARNING:” in all capital letters and bold print.
  • One of the short sentences for the endpoint, each of which names a chemical and points to www.P65Warnings.ca.gov. The shapes include “Cancer risk from exposure to [name of chemical]. See www.P65Warnings.ca.gov.” and “Can expose you to [name of chemical], a carcinogen. See www.P65Warnings.ca.gov.”, plus the reproductive-harm and combined-endpoint lines in § 25603(b)(3).

The same PDF’s § 25603(c) says a product manufactured and labeled prior to 1 January 2028 may use the earlier content regardless of when the product is sold to a consumer. Those earlier lines, as written there, use “WARNING:” and a short endpoint such as “Cancer -- www.P65Warnings.ca.gov.”, “Reproductive Harm -- www.P65Warnings.ca.gov.”, or “Cancer and Reproductive Harm -- www.P65Warnings.ca.gov.” Confirm the operative subsection before you treat an older line as still in the safe harbor.
