France places of manufacture vs Annex I

France places of manufacture vs EU fibre names (Annex I): two separate textile duties on Shopify

FibreLedger Textile Labels (Shopify App Store) is a Shopify app that records French places of manufacture for online textile listings, checks every fibre name against the official Annex I catalogue in EN, DE and FR, and flags trade names such as Lycra or Tencel that are not lawful fibre names. The listing also describes recording French origin and environmental-impact information and retaining EPR registration and audit records. It does not decide who is obligated, certify that a listing meets those duties, or provide legal advice.

Job: keep French places of manufacture and Annex I fibre names as two separate duties on the same textile offer — recording origin is not a fibre-composition substitute.

Two French layers — online display is not the AGEC fiche

Two instruments sit on French textile offers. They are not the same duty, and neither is the Annex I fibre-name catalogue.

Online display — L. 541-9-1-2 (LOI n° 2026-602)

LOI n° 2026-602 of 8 July 2026, article 2, inserts Code de l'environnement L. 541-9-1-2. The article reads:

« Les lieux de fabrication du produit mentionné au 11° de l'article L. 541-10-1 vendu en ligne doivent être portés à la connaissance du consommateur de manière claire et lisible sur la plateforme numérique, en caractères d'une taille égale à celle de l'indication du prix et à proximité de celui-ci. »

For a product mentioned in L. 541-10-1 11° sold online, the places of manufacture must be made known to the consumer on the digital platform, clearly and legibly, in characters the same size as the price indication, and next to that price. The ministry AGEC FAQ (November 2025) table lists 11° as textile clothing, household linen, and footwear (produits textiles d'habillement, linge, chaussures). Article 2 names no later start date of its own. The ministry page on the 8 July 2026 law records publication in the Journal officiel on 9 July 2026; the default entry into force is the following day, 10 July 2026 (Code civil, art. 1). The App Store listing records the feature as “French places of manufacture, in force since 10 July 2026, article quoted.” Confirm the live French text, not this page.

AGEC traçabilité fiche — Décret n° 2022-748 / R. 541-228

Décret n° 2022-748 of 29 April 2022 (inserted as then-R. 541-221 X; the ministry FAQ now cites R. 541-228) is a different instrument. Traçabilité is the country where each step is mainly carried out, when that step exists: weaving (tissage), dyeing and printing, making-up (confection); footwear uses stitching (piquage), assembly (montage), and finishing (finition). The FAQ treats tissage as weaving, knitting, or obtaining a non-woven cloth. That information is provided electronically at the time of purchase on a dedicated site or page titled as a product environmental-qualities fiche. The FAQ’s cumulative thresholds for producers, importers, and other placers on the French market are: from 1 January 2023, more than €50M turnover and at least 25,000 units; from 1 January 2024, more than €20M and at least 10,000 units; from 1 January 2025, more than €10M and at least 10,000 units a year. L. 541-9-1-2 does not repeat those thresholds. This page does not decide whether a shop sits above them.

“Lieux de fabrication” — the online-display article does not define the phrase

L. 541-9-1-2 uses “lieux de fabrication” and does not define it. A prudent reading often aligns those places with the AGEC step countries above. Counsel decides whether that alignment is the right reading for a given shop. This page does not.
