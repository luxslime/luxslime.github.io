Article 14 deadlines

Article 14 clocks are not the same job as an ENISA draft pack or an open-gap checklist

Direct answer. Article 14 applies from 11 September 2026: for actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents, manufacturers submit an early warning within 24 hours of becoming aware, a notification within 72 hours of becoming aware, and a final report no later than 14 days after a corrective or mitigating measure is available (vulnerabilities) or within one month after submission of the incident notification (severe incidents). Article 14 clocks (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847)

Art. 14 requires manufacturers to notify actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents to the coordinating CSIRT and ENISA via the single reporting platform. The clocks in the text are:

  • Early warning: without undue delay and in any event within 24 hours of becoming aware (Art. 14(2)(a); Art. 14(4)(a)).
  • Notification: without undue delay and in any event within 72 hours of becoming aware (Art. 14(2)(b); Art. 14(4)(b)).
  • Final report: no later than 14 days after a corrective or mitigating measure is available, for an actively exploited vulnerability (Art. 14(2)(c)); within one month after submission of the incident notification, for a severe incident (Art. 14(4)(c)).

Article 14 applies from 11 September 2026 (Art. 71). Source: Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 on EUR-Lex. This page is not legal advice.

Clocks vs packs vs gaps

Open record gaps ask what is missing before an incident. Encrypted ENISA SRP draft packs prepare a filing. Article 14 deadlines answer when the clock runs after an incident is in play.

How to track Article 14 incident deadlines

  1. Open the product duty file in CRALedger.
  2. Record the Article 14 incident deadline fields the checklist expects.
  3. Keep role checklists and open gaps current so the clock is not running on an empty file.
  4. Use ENISA draft packs when you are preparing a filing — the deadline tracker does not submit to ENISA.
