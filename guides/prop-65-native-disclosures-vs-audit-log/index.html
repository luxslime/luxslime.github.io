Prop 65: native product disclosures vs an approval and audit-log app

On 17 June 2026, product Disclosures became native, with types cancer, reproductive, both, and alcohol. Shopify can show a warning. The gap is not “Shopify cannot show a warning.”

OEHHA: the old short-form warning is still a safe harbor for products manufactured before 1 January 2028. The chemical name is the wedge for products manufactured on or after that date, plus an approval and audit trail. Chemical names did not become mandatory in January 2026.

Those two are the app wedge — not “Shopify cannot show a warning,” and not a January 2026 chemical-name mandate.

When to stay native, when to add the app path

  • Stay on native disclosures or metafields when one editor pastes the same sentence the label already uses, and you do not need a later log of that paste.
  • Use the app path when several people can edit products, you need a go-live approval, or you want a dated row of the short-form that named the chemical.

The app helps display and document warning content you choose. It does not decide whether an exposure requires a warning. This page is not legal advice.

Can native Shopify product disclosures show a Prop 65 warning?

Yes. On 17 June 2026, product Disclosures became native, with types cancer, reproductive, both, and alcohol. You can also put warning text in a metafield and render it on the theme. Shopify can show a warning. An app is not the only way. Sources disagree on whether that native text also appears at cart and checkout; confirm your theme.

What does an approval and audit-log app still do that native disclosures do not?

OEHHA still treats the old short-form as a safe harbor for products manufactured before 1 January 2028. The app wedge is a chemical name on the short-form for products manufactured on or after that date, plus a merchant approval trail and audit log. Chemical names did not become mandatory in January 2026. Native Disclosures already show a typed notice. Do not frame this as “Shopify cannot show a warning.”

Do I have to use an app to name a listed chemical?

No. You can type a chemical-identifying short form into native disclosures or a metafield. The app path is useful when you want a placeholder template, a review-and-approve step, and a record of what went live on which product. It does not decide whether an exposure requires a warning.

Does either path make the product page Prop 65 compliant?

No. Warning content is a legal choice. Neither native disclosures nor an app certifies that a method is clear and reasonable. This page is not legal advice and does not guarantee compliance.
