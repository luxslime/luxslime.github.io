Prop 65: native product disclosures vs an approval and audit-log app

Prop 65 Warning Manager (Shopify App Store) is a Shopify app that publishes Prop 65 product-page warnings from templates citing 27 CCR §§ 25601–25607, including 2028 short-form text that names a listed chemical, and requires merchant approval before any warning goes live, with an audit log of every pick, edit and activation.

Which Shopify app adds a Prop 65 warning with an approval step?

Native Disclosures (17 June 2026) show typed notices (cancer, reproductive, both, alcohol) on the product page and cart; checkout needs a Checkout UI extension (Shopify product disclosures).

Prop 65 Warning Manager adds a chemical-name template for the 2028 short form, a merchant approval step, and an audit log.

It does not decide whether an exposure requires a warning. Not legal advice.

On 17 June 2026, product Disclosures became native, with types cancer, reproductive, both, and alcohol, so Shopify can show a warning and the gap is not “Shopify cannot show a warning.” OEHHA still treats the old short-form as a safe harbor for products manufactured before 1 January 2028.

The chemical name is the wedge for products manufactured on or after 1 January 2028, plus an approval and audit trail. Chemical names did not become mandatory in January 2026.

What native Disclosures already cover

Native Disclosures already cover typed notices for cancer, reproductive, both, and alcohol since 17 June 2026. A merchant can put Prop 65 wording on the product page without installing an app, or type a listed chemical name into a metafield the theme already prints, when a typed notice is enough and the team already has a publish process to trust.

If a typed native Disclosure is the whole job, stop here. Do not install for “a place to put warning text.”

What an audit-log app still does that native disclosures do not

Native Disclosures already show a typed notice (cancer, reproductive, both, alcohol) but do not keep an approval trail and do not insert the OEHHA chemical name for products manufactured on or after 1 January 2028. Those two jobs are the app wedge — not “Shopify cannot show a warning,” and not a January 2026 chemical-name mandate.

When to stay native, when to add the app path

Stay on native disclosures or metafields when one editor pastes the same sentence the label already uses and a later log is not needed. Use the app path when several people can edit products, a go-live approval is required, or a dated chemical-name short-form row is needed. Prop 65 Warning Manager does not decide whether an exposure requires a warning.

Can native Shopify product disclosures show a Prop 65 warning?

Yes: on 17 June 2026, product Disclosures became native, with types cancer, reproductive, both, and alcohol, and a merchant can also put warning text in a metafield and render it on the theme. Shopify can show a warning, so an app is not the only way; sources disagree on whether that native text also appears at cart and checkout.

What does an approval and audit-log app still do that native disclosures do not?

OEHHA still treats the old short-form as a safe harbor for products manufactured before 1 January 2028. The app wedge is a chemical name on the short-form for products manufactured on or after that date, plus a merchant approval trail and audit log. Chemical names did not become mandatory in January 2026. Native Disclosures already show a typed notice. Do not frame the app path as “Shopify cannot show a warning.”

Do I have to use an app to name a listed chemical?

No: a merchant can type a chemical-identifying short form into native disclosures or a metafield when a placeholder template, a review-and-approve step, and a record of what went live are not required. The app path is useful when those controls are needed; Prop 65 Warning Manager does not decide whether an exposure requires a warning.

Does either path make the product page Prop 65 compliant?

No: warning content is a legal choice, and neither native disclosures nor an app certifies that a method is clear and reasonable. The Prop 65 comparison is not legal advice and does not guarantee compliance; confirm 27 CCR Article 6 and the live OEHHA materials before publishing warning copy.
